TDS on sale of immovable property

A quiet but compulsory step in almost every property purchase above ₹50 lakh — one that buyers often discover too late. Updated for the Income-tax Act, 2025.

1The basics

What is TDS on sale of property?

Governed by Section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 3(i) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 — successor to the erstwhile Section 194-IA of the Income-tax Act, 1961.

In plain terms

When a buyer purchases land (other than agricultural land) or a building or part of a building for ₹50 lakh or more, the buyer must deduct tax at source before paying the seller, and deposit it with the government on the seller’s behalf.

How much, and on what value

  • Deducted at 1% of the sale consideration or the stamp duty value, whichever is higher.
  • “Consideration” can include club membership, parking, water/electricity facility charges and other amounts incidental to the transfer.
  • Agricultural land is specifically excluded.

Who is responsible

The buyer — not the seller or the registrar — must deduct, deposit, and certify, at the time of payment or credit, whichever is earlier.

2The transition

From the 1961 Act to the 2025 Act

The rule hasn’t changed. The number attached to it — and the form you file — has. What matters is the date of payment, not the date on your sale agreement.

Before 1 April 2026

Section 194-IA, 1961 Act

Filed as Form 26QB, certificate issued as Form 16B.

On or after 1 April 2026

Section 393(1), 2025 Act

Filed as Form 141, Schedule B — one consolidated statement that also absorbs the erstwhile 26QC, 26QD and 26QE. Certificate is now Form 132, merging the old 16B, 16C, 16D and 16E.

3The clock

How long you have to deposit it

Two deadlines run back to back — one for the government, one for the seller.

01

Deduction

At the time of payment or credit to the seller — whichever is earlier.

02

Deposit

Within 30 days from the end of the month of deduction, via Form 141.

03

Certificate

Form 132 to the seller, within 15 days from the due date for filing Form 141.

Example: payment on 5 June 2026 → deposit due by 31 July 2026 → Form 132 due to the seller by around 15 August 2026.

If you miss it: interest applies per month of delay for failure to deduct or deposit, a late fee accrues per day for late filing, and a separate penalty applies for delayed certificate issuance — exact figures are best confirmed at the time of filing.

4Before you start

What you’ll need to get this done

Having these ready before filing avoids the back-and-forth that usually causes delays.

01

Aadhaar & PAN

Buyer and seller. A mismatch or missing PAN can trigger higher deduction requirements.

02

Seller’s address

As it should appear on the deduction certificate.

03

Property address

Matching the sale documents exactly.

04

Agreement / sale deed

Establishes transaction value, stamp duty value and payment schedule.

05

Payment details

Date, mode and amount of each instalment — every payment can trigger its own filing.

5The portal

The new TRACES portal — and its rough edges

Revamped alongside the new Act from 1 April 2026. The core process is sound; the transition period isn’t always smooth.

Old bookmarks redirect unexpectedly

Returning users often land on an unfamiliar homepage.

A fresh “Taxpayer” registration is required

Separate from the income-tax e-filing login.

Form 132 isn’t available on demand

Only generated after Form 141 is filed and processed.

One form, four old schedules

Picking the wrong schedule is a common filing error.

Corrections happen only on TRACES

Not on the e-filing portal used for the original filing.

Silent failures need manual follow-up

A missing filing requires a grievance — no automatic retry.

6Practical points

A checklist for buyers

Form 141 is the erstwhile Form 26QB — not the annual tax credit statement, now Form 168 (previously Form 26AS). Keeping that distinction clear avoids most avoidable errors.

01

Check the payment date

The date of payment, not the agreement date, decides which framework applies.

02

Compute on the higher value

1% applies on the higher of consideration and stamp duty value, including incidental charges.

03

Use the government portals

Statement submission, correction and certificate download are carried out on the official portals.

04

Treat instalments separately

For under-construction property, each instalment is a separate payment with its own deduction.

05

Note the due dates

Keep track of the deposit date and the certificate date for every payment.

06

Keep your records

Retain payment proofs, challan details and the certificate issued to the seller.

7A closing note

Thank you for reading this far. Property transactions carry enough weight without a compliance step catching you off guard — TDS here is small on paper, but it carries real deadlines, real penalties, and a genuinely new set of forms and portals.
