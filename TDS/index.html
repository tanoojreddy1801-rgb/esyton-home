A Guide to TDS on Sale of Immovable Property

1THE BASICS

What is TDS on Sale of Immovable Property?

A quiet but compulsory step in almost every property purchase above ₹50 lakh — one that buyers often discover too late.

Governed by Section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 3(i) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 — successor to the erstwhile Section 194-IA of the Income-tax Act, 1961.

In plain terms

When a buyer purchases immovable property — land (other than agricultural land) or a building or part of a building — for a consideration of ₹50 lakh or more, the buyer must deduct tax at source before paying the seller, and deposit it with the government on the seller's behalf.

How much, and on what value

  • Tax is deducted at 1% of the sale consideration or the stamp duty value, whichever is higher.
  • "Consideration" can include club membership, parking, water/electricity facility charges and other amounts incidental to the transfer.
  • Agricultural land is specifically excluded.

Who is responsible

The buyer — not the seller or the registrar — must deduct, deposit, and certify, at the time of payment or credit, whichever is earlier.

2THE TRANSITION

From the 1961 Act to the 2025 Act

The rule hasn't changed. The number attached to it — and the form you file — has.

What matters is the date of payment, not the date on your sale agreement.

Before 1 April 2026

Section 194-IA of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Filed as Form 26QB, certificate issued as Form 16B.

On or after 1 April 2026

Section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 3(i) of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Filed as Form 141, Schedule B — one consolidated statement that also absorbs the erstwhile 26QC, 26QD and 26QE. Certificate is now Form 132, merging the old 16B, 16C, 16D and 16E.

What stays the same

  • ₹50 lakh threshold · 1% rate on the higher value · agricultural land excluded · buyer deducts and deposits
An agreement signed in Feb 2026 with payment made in June 2026 falls under the new regime — Form 141, not Form 26QB.
3THE CLOCK

How Long You Have to Deposit It

Two deadlines run back to back — one for the government, one for the seller.

Deduction At the time of payment or credit to the seller — whichever is earlier.
Deposit with the government Within 30 days from the end of the month of deduction, via Form 141.
Certificate to the seller Form 132, within 15 days from the due date for filing Form 141.
Example: payment on 5 June 2026 → deposit due by 31 July 2026 → Form 132 due to the seller by around 15 August 2026.

If you miss it

Interest applies per month of delay for failure to deduct or deposit, a late fee accrues per day for late filing, and a separate penalty applies for delayed certificate issuance — exact figures are best confirmed at the time of filing.

4BEFORE YOU START

What You'll Need to Get This Done

Having these ready before filing avoids the back-and-forth that usually causes delays.

  • 1
    Aadhaar & PAN — buyer and sellerA mismatch or missing PAN can trigger higher deduction requirements.
  • 2
    Seller's addressAs it should appear on the deduction certificate.
  • 3
    Address of the propertyMatching the sale documents exactly.
  • 4
    Agreement to sell / sale deedEstablishes transaction value, stamp duty value and payment schedule.
  • 5
    Payment detailsDate, mode and amount of each instalment — every payment can trigger its own filing.
5THE PORTAL

The New TRACES Portal — and Its Rough Edges

Revamped alongside the new Act from 1 April 2026. The core process is sound; the transition period isn't always smooth.

  • Old bookmarks redirect unexpectedlyReturning users often land on an unfamiliar homepage.
  • A fresh "Taxpayer" registration is requiredSeparate from the income-tax e-filing login.
  • Form 132 isn't available on demandOnly generated after Form 141 is filed and processed.
  • One form, four old schedulesPicking the wrong schedule is a common filing error.
  • Corrections happen only on TRACESNot on the e-filing portal used for the original filing.
  • Silent failures need manual follow-upA missing filing requires a grievance — no automatic retry.
